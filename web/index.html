What we are doing

Rejection risk, read early and explained

Rejection is the main threat to a transplanted kidney, and creatinine often rises only after damage has started. CortexAI combines the signals transplant teams already collect into one forward-looking risk, shows which factors drive it, and ranks the recipients who need attention first.

How it works

Three signal layers, one risk the transplant team can act on

Immunogenetics

How foreign is the graft

Donor and recipient HLA at allele level, read as molecular mismatch rather than antigen counts.

  • A, B, C, DRB1, DQB1 side by side
  • Class II eplet mismatch, PIRCHE-II
  • cPRA, preformed and de novo DSA by locus
Drug exposure

Is immunosuppression holding

Tacrolimus troughs against the target corridor for each post-transplant phase.

  • Time in therapeutic range
  • Intrapatient variability, stable phase
  • Under-exposure flags
Graft injury

Is the graft being hurt now

Non-invasive markers that move before, or alongside, creatinine.

  • dd-cfDNA fraction trend
  • eGFR (CKD-EPI 2021) slope, UPCR
  • BK viremia, Banff biopsy history

Clinical basis

Built on markers transplant centres already use

Default thresholds come from published work, and every centre can tune them. Today the model is trained on a synthetic cohort; it needs validation on real recipients before clinical use.

dd-cfDNA ≥ 1%

The donor-derived cell-free DNA fraction most often used to separate normal from abnormal, with high negative predictive value for rejection.

Kant & Brennan, Transplant International 2022
DSA MFI ≥ 1,400

The donor-specific antibody cut-off inside the iBox prognostication system, which combines eGFR, proteinuria, DSA and Banff lesions.

Loupy et al., BMJ 2019
Class II eplets ≥ 14

Living-donor recipients above this load developed de novo class II DSA far more often (20.2% vs 3.4%).

Sakamoto et al., Frontiers in Immunology 2020
Tacrolimus CV > 30%

High intrapatient variability and time below range are associated with rejection and graft loss; published cut-offs span roughly 23–40%.

Benning et al., Frontiers in Immunology 2026
